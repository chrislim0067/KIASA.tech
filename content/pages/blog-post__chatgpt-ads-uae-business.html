ChatGPT Has Ads Now. Not in Singapore — and That Is the Useful Part

← Back to Blog

On 9 February 2026, OpenAI began showing ads inside ChatGPT. Six months later the pilot covers the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, Mexico, Brazil, Japan, South Korea and — from late August — 31 European countries.

Read that list again. There is no Gulf market on it.

That absence is the most useful thing about this story for a business in Singapore, and it is the part almost nobody writing about it will lead with. You cannot buy these ads here yet. What you can do is understand what is being built, because the way it works has implications for your website that arrive long before the ad inventory does.

What actually shipped

The mechanics matter, so here they are from OpenAI's own pages rather than from commentary about them.

Ads appear only on the Free and Go tiers. Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Education remain ad-free. If you pay for ChatGPT, you have probably never seen one, which is worth knowing before you assume your customers have.

Ad selection reads the conversation. OpenAI states that during the test it decides which ad to show by "matching ads submitted by advertisers with the topic of your conversation, your past chats, and past interactions with ads." Its own example: if you are researching recipes, you may see ads for meal kits or grocery delivery.

Advertisers see aggregates, not conversations. "Advertisers do not have access to your chats, chat history, memories, or personal details," OpenAI says. They receive counts — views, clicks. This is a meaningfully different bargain from the one most ad platforms offer, and it is the reason the targeting is contextual rather than built on a profile the advertiser can inspect.

Answers are held separate from ads. OpenAI's stated principle is that "ads do not influence the answers ChatGPT gives you," and that ads are "always clearly labeled as sponsored and visually separated from the organic answer." Whether that separation holds under commercial pressure over years is a fair question to keep asking. It is not a question anyone can answer from six months of pilot data.

There are exclusions. No ads in accounts OpenAI believes belong to under-18s, and ads are "not eligible to appear near sensitive or regulated topics like health, mental health or politics."

Users can turn them off. Upgrade to Plus or Pro, or opt out on the Free tier in exchange for fewer daily messages. That second option is unusual and tells you something: OpenAI is pricing attention against usage rather than forcing the trade.

The buying side is now a real platform

In May 2026 OpenAI added cost-per-click bidding and a beta self-serve Ads Manager, alongside access through agency partners. That is the moment a pilot stops being an experiment and starts being an ad network: CPC bidding, measurement tooling, and a way for a business to buy without a direct relationship.

In the European launch, OpenAI is explicit that access comes first through its own solutions team, agency partners and technology partners, with self-service "to follow later this summer." The pattern in each new market is the same — managed first, self-serve after.

Why this is different from search advertising

The interesting shift is not that AI has ads. It is where in the decision the ad sits.

Search advertising works on keywords — compressed, abbreviated statements of intent. Someone types "web development company dubai" and a platform infers a great deal from three words. OpenAI's framing of the opportunity is that people using ChatGPT do the opposite: they "explain what they want to accomplish, what matters to them, and the constraints they face."

That is a much richer signal, and it lands earlier. A person comparing options in a conversation has not yet written the search query. By the time they do, the comparison has often already happened.

Which raises the question a Singapore business should actually be asking, and it is not about ad budgets.

What to do while you wait

You cannot buy ChatGPT ads in Singapore today. You can do the thing that matters more, and it is unglamorous: make your business legible to a system that reads rather than crawls.

When someone asks an assistant to compare web agencies in Singapore, or to explain what a project like theirs should cost, the assistant answers from what it can read and understand. That is not a new discipline requiring a new retainer. It is the same set of things that have always separated a useful site from a brochure:

  • Answer real questions in plain language. A page that explains what a build includes, what it excludes and roughly what it costs is readable by a person and by a model. A page of adjectives is readable by neither.
  • Publish the specifics you are tempted to withhold. Scope, timelines, what you do not do. Vagueness is not a negotiating position when the comparison is being made without you in the room.
  • Structure the content properly. Real headings, real lists, real semantic markup. This is the same work that has always helped search engines, and it has not become obsolete.
  • Keep it fast and accessible. A page that fails to render or takes eight seconds is a page that contributes nothing to anyone's answer.

If that sounds like a description of ordinary good practice rather than a novel AI strategy, that is the point. We wrote about the same underlying idea when the free and frontier tiers split apart: most of what people sell as AI readiness is work that was always worth doing.

The thing to be sceptical about

Expect to be offered "ChatGPT Ads management" in this market shortly, if you have not been already. Before you buy it, ask which market the inventory is in. As of today OpenAI has not announced availability in Singapore, Saudi Arabia or anywhere else in the Gulf, and it has published no performance data for any market — no benchmark CPCs, no conversion rates, no case studies with numbers attached.

Anyone quoting you a ChatGPT Ads CPC for Singapore is quoting an estimate. That may be an honest estimate, but you should know which one it is before it appears in a media plan. The same discipline we argued for in performance marketing across the GCC applies here: a channel with no published data is a channel you test small, not one you shift budget into on a promise.

What we would actually watch

Three signals, in order of how much they should change your plans:

  1. A GCC market appearing in an OpenAI announcement. Not a reseller's claim — OpenAI's own rollout post. Every expansion so far has been announced there first.
  2. Self-serve Ads Manager opening in a market near ours. Managed access means agencies and large advertisers; self-serve is when a mid-sized Singapore business could realistically run something.
  3. Published measurement. OpenAI has shipped CPC bidding and "expanded measurement tools." Until someone can report attributed outcomes rather than views and clicks, this is a brand and discovery channel, not a performance one.

None of those have happened here yet. That is not a reason to ignore the shift — it is a reason to spend the interval on the part you control, which is whether your own site can answer for you when you are not there.
